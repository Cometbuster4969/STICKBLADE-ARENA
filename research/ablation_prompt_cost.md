## What this does and does not show

- **Does show**: what each field costs to send, every turn, forever. A field that costs 12% of every prompt has to earn that.
- **Does NOT show**: whether a field improves play. That needs provider traffic — run `python3 tools/run_ablation.py matches --model <id>` when keys and budget are available.

## Reading of the numbers (2026-09-09)

The one result worth acting on: **the ranged-combat hint block is ~25 % of
every prompt and is only meaningful for the bow.**

`no_arena_modifier` removes `arena`, `ranged_hint.per_shot` and
`ranged_hint.gravity_scale`, and saves 74 of 294 tokens (25.3 %). The three
`per_shot` entries are flight-time/drop tables for `draw_shot`,
`quick_shot` and `high_arc_shot` — none of which exist for sword, dagger,
spear or flail. Every melee match in the benchmark has been paying to send
bow aiming tables every turn.

That is a **hypothesis, not a fix**. Emitting `per_shot` only when the
match weapon is `bow` would change the state the models see, which means a
`PROMPT_VERSION` bump and a break in Elo comparability with everything
recorded so far (`brains.py:140-163` sets out the protocol). Doing that
silently to save tokens would trade a small, certain cost saving for a
large, certain loss of comparability — the wrong trade. It is queued as a
prompt-version decision, not applied here.

Two smaller notes:

- **`normalized_coords` costs *more* (+4.2 %)**, because `0.4167` is longer
  than `520`. If normalized coordinates ever turn out to help models, they
  cost ~12 tokens per turn to say so.
- **`no_velocity` saves only 3.4 %** — velocity is cheap. Whatever the
  behavioural sweep says about velocity, cost is not the argument.
